Question:
A parent of a two-year-old noticed sudden swelling on the back of the child's neck after daycare. The daycare suspected an allergic reaction after breakfast, but the child has no known allergies. The swelling stayed in the same spot for several hours. About two weeks earlier, the child had a mosquito bite in that same area that was almost healed. The child does not seem very itchy or in pain. What could be causing the swelling?

Answer:
Several possibilities could explain sudden swelling in this situation. A delayed allergic reaction is one option, even if the child has not reacted before. Re-irritation of the old mosquito bite is another possibility, especially if the area was scratched or rubbed. A mild secondary skin infection could also develop around a healing bite. Contact irritation from wipes, soap, lotion, clothing, or detergent is also worth considering.

If the child is otherwise acting normally and has no fever or spreading symptoms, the situation may not be serious. Still, parents should watch for worsening swelling, redness, pain, drainage, or fever. If those signs appear, or the swelling does not improve, medical evaluation is recommended.
